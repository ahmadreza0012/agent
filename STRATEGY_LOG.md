# Strategy Experiment Log

This document records all experiments conducted on the crypto bot's trading strategies.
**Objective**: To meticulously track parameters, data, and results to prevent repeating mistakes and identify robust market patterns.

---

## Experiment 1: Initial Backtest (30-Day Data)
- **Strategy**: SMA Crossover
- **Parameters**: Short Window=10, Long Window=30, Position Size=100%, Fee=0.25%, Slippage=0.1%
- **Data**: ~30 days of BTC/IRT hourly candles
- **Results**: Total Return: -13.35%, Total Trades: 15, Win Rate: 20%, Max Drawdown: -14.79%
- **Status**: ✅ Reliable

- **Status**: ✅ Reliable

## Experiment 3: Backtest on 180-Day Data

- **Results**: Total Return: -34.46%, Total Trades: 84, Win Rate: 25%, Max Drawdown: -42.11%
- **Status**: ✅ Reliable (Independently re-verified via direct user execution).

### Experiment 3b: SMA with Alternative Windows (9, 21)

## Experiment 4: Grid Search - Flawed Version
- **Discovered Bug**: The Grid Search loop shared the input DataFrame between iterations. Because `apply_moving_average_strategy` lacked `df.copy()`, signals from the previous iteration leaked into and affected the subsequent ones.
- **Flawed Results**: Reported 137 trades for (9,21) instead of the actual 116.
- **Status**: ❌ Invalidated

---

## Experiment 5: Grid Search - Fixed Version (Isolated Subprocesses)
- **Changes**: Executed each parameter combination in an isolated Python subprocess.
- **Results**: Confirmed that (9,21) with 116 trades and (10,30) with 84 trades are the true, verified figures.
- **Status**: ✅ Reliable

## Experiment 7: Bollinger Bands Strategy
- **Parameters**: window=20, num_std=2.0
- **Full Period Results (180 Days)**: Total Return: **-56.78%**, Total Trades: **39**, Win Rate: **33.3%**, Max Drawdown: **-59.22%**, Sharpe Ratio: -0.624
- **Analysis**: This was the worst-performing strategy among all 4 tested—contradicting the initial hypothesis that a mean-reversion strategy would excel in a sideways/ranging market.
- **Status**: ✅ Reliable (Verified via direct execution; an earlier version generated by Qwen was discarded as it contained completely fabricated metrics).

---

## Experiment 8: Walk-Forward Analysis (RSI Strategy)
- **Methodology**: Split the 180-day data into two equal halves:
  - **TRAIN**: 2026-01-13 to 2026-04-13 (2,160 candles)
  - **TEST**: 2026-04-13 to 2026-07-12 (2,160 candles)
  - Optimized parameters on TRAIN, then tested the best-performing combination on the unseen TEST data without re-optimization.
- **TRAIN Results (Best of 6 combinations)**: (14, 20, 80) → 2 trades, Return: +16.89%, Win Rate: 50%, Max Drawdown: -8.16%, Sharpe: 0.354
- **TEST Results (Unseen data, same parameters)**: 1 trade, Return: **-13.99%**, Win Rate: 100% (single trade, but closed at a loss), Max Drawdown: -27.76%, Sharpe: -0.243
- **Status**: ✅ Reliable (Direct user verification).
- **Key Takeaway**: The severe performance drop from TRAIN to TEST is a classic signature of **Overfitting**. The (14, 20, 80) parameters had merely fit the noise of a specific period rather than capturing an exploitable market anomaly.

---

## Executive Summary

Across all 4 tested strategies (SMA with 2 variations, RSI with 6 variations, and Bollinger Bands) executed on 180 days of hourly BTC/IRT data, **not a single combination demonstrated a reliable, statistically significant edge**. The only seemingly positive result (RSI 14/20/80 at +0.83%) was thoroughly debunked by Walk-Forward Analysis.

**Key Lesson**: Single-period backtesting is never sufficient. Out-of-Sample / Walk-Forward testing must become the project standard, even when full-period backtests look promising.
